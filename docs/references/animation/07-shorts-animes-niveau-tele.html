animated channels make so much money just by posting on youtube.

kids stories, old legends, little anime scenes. you scroll past them every day and they do millions of views. most of them have no face to represent the channel.

the big problem was making them.

an animator costs real money for every single short. doing it yourself costs days. and the ai video tools i tried gave me the same thing every time: faces that melt, hands with seven fingers, a character who changes clothes between two shots.

slop. you can see it in one second, and so can everyone else.

so i built a skill where claude makes the whole film, in code.

What It Actually Does

you say what the film is about. one paragraph is enough.

[top model] writes the story, draws every frame in code and renders a vertical video, 1080 x 1920, with the captions landing on the spoken word.

no video model. no stock footage. the same scene comes out the same way every time you run it, so your character doesn't turn into someone else halfway through.

it goes in five steps:

  1. script. the story, shot by shot, with the second each shot starts
  2. sound. the voice with a time for every word, music under it
  3. scene. every frame drawn in code
  4. stills. it looks at its own frames and repaints the weakest shots
  5. render. a 1080p video with captions on the word

It Watches Its Own Film, Then Fixes It

this is the part that surprised me.

on the quiet blade, the first pass had problems. the old master was hiding the swordsman behind him. the draw was a flat figure with scan lines. the last shot was two tiny figures over an empty half of the screen.

nobody told it. it read its own stills three times, saw what was weak and repainted those shots before it rendered.

that's what an editor does. watch it, hate something, fix it. except it doesn't get tired at 2am.

13+ Looks In The Box

you pick a look and it already knows the recipe: palette, camera moves, caption font, what should move in the background.

painted, ink wash, talking anime, noir, papercraft, chalk, low-poly, kinetic type and more. there are also two looks that animate pictures you bring.

and it creates characters that stay the same from the first shot to the last.

How You Can Make Money With It

this is where it gets interesting. the expensive part of animated content was always the making. now the making is a paragraph.

→ faceless animated channels. kids stories, bedtime stories, history, myths, bible stories, anime-style scenes. post them on youtube shorts, tiktok and reels, and once you qualify, the monetization programs pay you on the views.

→ one niche, every day. the channels that grow post a lot, in one style, on one topic. before, posting daily meant paying for daily animation. now it's a prompt.

→ sell short ads to local businesses. a bakery, a gym, a dentist. most of them have never had an animated ad because an animator was never in the budget. a 20 second animated short with their story is an easy thing to sell.

→ do it as a service. creators and agencies already pay editors every month. you can deliver animated shorts in their style, on repeat.

→ animated explainers for apps and products. the skill has looks for data reels, kinetic type and 3d product demos.

→ clipping and ugc campaigns. brands pay for views on short videos. an animated short stands out in a feed full of talking heads.

the cost side is simple. an animator usually quotes hundreds of dollars for one short, often more (typical market rates, not a promise). this runs on your claude subscription, plus elevenlabs if you want a voice

How To Start

  1. get it for free
  2. unzip it, type claude, then hi
  3. say what the film is about

you need claude code with [top model], node.js 18 or newer, ffmpeg, and chrome or edge

the voice uses your own elevenlabs key. without one it makes the film to your own audio, to music, or silent.
