Rewind 1: The picture

What you see is a flipbook. Fifteen seconds of video is 900 still pictures shown very fast.

The twist: no person drew them. Kevin Ngo put it in one line: "[advanced model] drew every frame of this animation in JavaScript." 633K+ views.

So who drew the frames? A program. Rewind again.

Rewind 2: The code

[advanced model] can only write text. So it writes a small program with one job: draw any moment of your video. Ask for second 7.3, and you get the exact picture of second 7.3.

Then your computer runs that program 900 times, takes a screenshot each time and glues them into an MP4.

This is why it's so powerful. A fix is just a sentence: "Logo half a second earlier." Done.

To see this layer on your own screen, you need Claude Code. It's the Claude that can run programs on your computer. The normal chat can write the animation, but only Claude Code can render it, look at it and fix it.

Ten minutes, once:

# Mac (on Linux, use apt instead of brew)
brew install node ffmpeg
mkdir my-studio && cd my-studio && npm init -y
npm i -D playwright && npx playwright install chromium

# start Claude Code on [advanced model]
claude --model [advanced model]

Inside Claude Code, type /model and set effort to xhigh for new videos. Medium is fine for small fixes.

No terminal at all? On Pro or above, open any Claude chat, pick Output > Design and ask for an animated page. Team and Enterprise also get Output > Motion, which exports MP4.

Rewind 3: The soundtrack

Unmute most AI videos and the magic breaks. The cuts miss the beat.

The good ones are built to the music. Leon Lin went further and built earshot: a library of 201 sounds made entirely with code, plus a tool that times each sound to the exact frame. 7K+ people saved it.

Two rules that cover almost everything:

  • Have a song? Ask Claude to find its beats and put every big moment on a beat.
  • No song? Ask it to make the music and the clicks in code, on the same timeline as the picture.

Telling a story? Record the voice yourself. A real human voice earns more trust than any effect.

Rewind 4: The rules

Here's why most AI videos look the same: centered text on a gradient, everything fades in, a logo at the end. That's [advanced model] with no rules.

The viral ones had rules. Usually a short file called CLAUDE.md that Claude Code reads before every job. Here's one you can copy into your project folder:

# My studio rules

Every video is a pure function of time: window.seek(t) draws the frame.
No CSS transitions, no timers, no Math.random. Same code = same video.

Something new on screen every 2-4 seconds.
Motion uses springs (small overshoot, then settle), never plain fades.
One accent color. Two fonts max.

BANNED: centered title on a gradient, everything fading in,
labels in the corners, frame borders, glow, particle explosions,
random camera drift, a whoosh on every cut.

Before showing me anything: put all frames on one image, look at it,
score yourself 1-10, fix the 3 worst problems, repeat until 8+.

One trap: writing "avoid the generic AI look" doesn't work. It just swaps one default for another. Name exactly what you ban.

Rewind 5: The reference

Rules say what not to do. A reference says what to aim for.

Without one, [advanced model] goes default. With one, it borrows the pace, the fonts and the transitions. Cut a video you love into frames:

ffmpeg -i ref.mp4 -vf "fps=2,scale=540:-2" frames/ref-%04d.jpg

Then paste:

Look at every frame in ./frames. Treat this video as a style, not as content.
Write style_guide.md: colors (hex), fonts, how long each shot lasts,
how shots change, how the camera moves, how text appears.
Then write shotlist.md for a [LENGTH]-second video about [MY TOPIC]
in this style. Never copy its characters, logos or story.
Show me both files and wait for my OK before writing any code.

The best reference is your own stuff. A folder of your photos or old designs is a style nobody else can copy.

Rewind 6: The words

Finally, the prompt. The part everyone thinks is the whole thing.

Every viral post says "one prompt". Here's what that actually looked like, from smallest to biggest.

One line. The most famous prompt of the trend. Stephan Livera used it word for word: 1.5M+ views, 11K+ saves.

make a dynamic 15-second motion graphics video that shows what an incredible motion designer you are, like it's your showreel for a résumé. go all out.

It works because "showreel" is a format [advanced model] already knows, and there are no facts to get wrong. But Ajith ran the same line (536K+ views), then hundreds of people did, and the videos started to look alike. Use it to test your setup, not to post.

One paragraph. Tony Dinh, the founder of TypingMind, paid $1,000+ for a launch video a year ago. With [advanced model] he made one in under 30 minutes.

The difference is a link, real screenshots and music. Template:

Make a 20-second motion graphics video for [PRODUCT] ([URL]),
with the energy of a motion designer's showreel. Go all out.

1. Visit the site. Take real screenshots, the real logo, real text.
   Save them to ./assets and list what you found before animating.
   Never redraw the product from imagination.
2. Story, one beat every 2-4 seconds:
   - Hook: the problem in 5 huge words
   - The product appears and builds itself piece by piece
   - 3 features, each with a cursor doing a real click
   - One number that proves it works: [NUMBER]
   - Logo + [CALL TO ACTION]
3. Original music at 120 BPM, made in code. Clicks land on the beat.
4. Vertical 1080x1920. Show me all frames on one image before the final render.

One spec. The most saved prompt of the whole trend: 907K+ views, 19K+ saves. One shape never cuts: a button turns into a loader, then a player, then a chart, a cursor clicks through it, and the last frame matches the first so it loops forever. The full template is in the post.

A short version to start with:

One HTML file, 1080x1080, everything computed inside seek(t).
One shape that never cuts: it changes size, corner radius and color
through these states: [button, loader, checkmark, music player, slider, chart].
A cursor drives every change with real clicks.
120 BPM, something happens on every beat. Springs with a tiny overshoot.
The last frame equals the first, so it loops.
Banned: bouncy easing, glows, gradients, particles.
Show me the list of states on the beat grid before you build.

One night. Donald's "one prompt" from the intermission was about 9,500 characters, and Claude worked for 12 hours.

And Rexan Wong said what a lot of people felt: everyone says they made it with "one prompt", but his one-prompt video looked mid. 486K+ views on that confession.

So the words matter. But they're one layer out of seven. Which brings us to the layer most people skip.

Rewind 7: The second look

[advanced model] can see images. So before anyone else watches your video, make [advanced model] watch it.

Put the whole video on one picture, one frame per second:

ffmpeg -i out/video.mp4 -vf "fps=1,scale=240:-1,tile=8x8" -frames:v 1 contact.png

Then:

Open contact.png. You did not make this video.
You are the harshest motion director I know.
Score 1-10: the first 2 seconds, readability on a phone,
motion quality, variety, sound sync.
Find the 3 biggest problems with timestamps.
Fix only those. Re-render only those seconds. Show me the new image.
Repeat until every score is 8+.

This loop is the difference between "it's a bit mid" and a video people share.
